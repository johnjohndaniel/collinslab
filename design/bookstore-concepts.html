Four ways to be a small bookshop online

Each concept below is a cosier, more familiar alternative to the walk-through store. Every one keeps the same core: a few books chosen for you, real covers, the book you can pick up and leaf through, and buying through Bookshop.org. They differ in how you arrive, how you move around, and how much of a "place" the shop feels like. Blue tags mark the mechanics; the flow strips show the key screens in order.

B

The dollhouse

The whole shop as one hand-painted cutaway, like a dollhouse with the front wall off. Nooks are the sections. You never walk: you click a nook and lean in, and that shelf becomes a row of real covers you can scroll.

Mechanics

  • Click a nook: the camera eases in (a zoom, not a walk)
  • Each nook = one curated shelf of 12–20 books
  • Search and bag float quietly at the side

Good at

  • Cutest and most "mom-and-pop"
  • A real sense of place without game controls
  • Seasonal changes (snow on the roof, a pumpkin on the counter)

Watch out

  • Needs good illustration, the biggest art task
  • A fixed number of nooks: very large catalogues need search to carry them
C

Letters from the shop

The most personal version. The shopkeeper writes you a short letter with a few questions; a small stack of books arrives on the counter, each with a note on why it suits you. You flip through, set some aside, ask for more like one, and check out. Very few options at any moment.

Mechanics

  • Questions → stack of 5 → decide each
  • "More like this" refills the stack from that book's neighbours
  • Optional emails: "a new stack came in"

Good at

  • Most bespoke; fewest options at a time
  • Excellent on phones
  • Recommendation quality is the product

Watch out

  • Less browsing freedom: needs a quiet "see the whole shop" escape hatch
  • The notes must be good; generic notes would feel fake
D

Shelves as pages

The familiar layout of an online bookstore (sections, filters, search), except every list is drawn as a softly lit shelf: spines and face-out covers, a bookend, the odd shelf-talker. Hovering a book tilts it out; clicking pulls it into your hands.

Mechanics

  • Standard pages and URLs per section and search
  • Spines and face-outs mixed, so lists feel browsable
  • Hover tilt, then the 3D book on click

Good at

  • Handles a huge catalogue gracefully
  • Familiar, fast, easy to search and filter
  • Search engines can index every shelf

Watch out

  • Less of a "place"; charm comes from details
  • Spines carry less information than covers on small screens

Questions for you

Answer in chat or leave comments on this page; a word or two each is plenty.

Which concept should lead, and what would you borrow from the others?

For example: "B, with D's shelves for search" or "A, but with C's letter on the front page".

What should the shop look like?

  • Hand-illustrated (watercolour or gouache), like a picture book
  • Soft 3D, like a miniature model shop seen from a fixed angle
  • The current warm photo-real look, used more sparingly
  • A clean modern store with paper textures and handwriting

Whose voice is it?

  • A fictional shopkeeper with a name (and a cat)
  • You, as the curator: your own picks and notes
  • The shop itself, gently anonymous
  • Mostly quiet: let the covers speak

How much should the 3D book moment stay?

  • Central: every book can be picked up, turned over and opened
  • Occasional: a quick tilt-and-flip preview, not a full scene
  • Gone: ordinary product pages, but beautifully made

What matters most for the next version?

  • Phones first
  • A much larger selection with search
  • A real-feeling bag and checkout
  • Recommendations that feel personal

Who is it for?

Just you and friends, a public site you'd like strangers to enjoy, or the start of a real business? This changes how much we invest in buying, accounts and the catalogue.
